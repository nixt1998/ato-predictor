I. Introduction

Arsenic trioxide (ATO) is a first-line agent for acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL) and has markedly improved patient survival. However, ATO may induce cardiotoxicity during treatment, including QT-interval prolongation and arrhythmias, which in severe cases can lead to life-threatening cardiac events. After entering the body, arsenic undergoes a series of methylation reactions, and the distribution of its metabolites is closely associated with the occurrence of cardiotoxicity.

This report is based on a machine-learning model that integrates the patient's arsenic-metabolism indicators (iAs, MMA, DMA), methylation capacity indices (PMI, SMI) and concomitant-medication status to estimate the probability of ATO-related cardiotoxicity, providing a quantitative reference to support clinical decision-making.

VII. Precautions

Model Characteristics
This report is generated by a machine-learning model. It provides a risk-probability estimate for clinical reference and should be interpreted together with a comprehensive clinical evaluation, laboratory tests, imaging studies and the physician's professional judgment.
Clinical Application
The model is derived from statistical analysis of existing data and cannot capture every individual variation. Treatment decisions should be made by the physician according to the patient's specific condition, medical history and other relevant factors.
Monitoring
Regardless of the predicted risk level, ECG monitoring and electrolyte testing are recommended throughout the treatment course, with particular attention to changes in the QTc interval; adjust the plan promptly if abnormalities are detected.
Medication Management
When concomitant medications are used, evaluate potential drug interactions carefully. Their overall impact should be assessed comprehensively in the context of the patient's complete medication profile and clinical status.
